# Importing transactions

Almost every bank lets you download your transactions as a CSV or Excel file from its website
(look for "Download", "Export" or "Statements"). FlyBudget can import those files into any
account. It's
free, works with every bank, and nothing but the file is involved. If you'd rather have
transactions arrive by themselves, [connect a bank](./bank-sync) instead.

## Importing a file

1. Open the account the transactions belong to, and choose **Import CSV**.
2. Drop the file on the window, or click to pick it.
3. **Map the columns.** FlyBudget reads the file's headers and guesses what each column holds.
   Check the guesses, and change any that are wrong: **Date**, **Payee**, **Amount**,
   **Inflow**, **Outflow**, **Notes** or **Skip** (and, for a statement in two currencies,
   **Amount in pesos**, **Amount in dollars** or **Currency**: see
   [Credit cards](#credit-cards)). The first rows are shown underneath so you can see that it
   looks right. You need a date and either an amount or inflow/outflow columns.

   <Screenshot name="import-csv" alt="Mapping the columns of a CSV file" dialog />

   Under the columns, check **Dates** and **Decimals** (see
   [Dates and decimals](#dates-and-decimals) below). FlyBudget shows how the first row reads, so
   you can tell at a glance that the day and the amount are right.

4. **Preview.** Every row is listed with its date, payee and amount. Rows you already have are
   marked **duplicate** and unticked. Untick anything else you don't want.

   <Screenshot name="import-csv-preview" alt="The preview before importing" dialog />

5. Choose **Import**. FlyBudget says how many transactions it imported and how many it skipped.

Your [rules](./rules) run on every imported transaction, so they can rename
`SQ *DAILY GRIND 1142` to _Daily Grind Coffee_ and put it in _Coffee Shops_. FlyBudget keeps the
bank's original text as the **imported description**, which rules can match on too.

## Duplicates

FlyBudget remembers every row it imports, so importing a file that overlaps one you imported
before is safe: the rows you already have are skipped. Two identical rows in the same file (two
coffees at the same price on the same day) are treated as two purchases.

Transactions you entered by hand aren't matched against an import. If you enter transactions by
hand _and_ import files for the same account, check the preview for rows you already have.

## What FlyBudget understands

- **Dates**: `2026-01-05`, `05/01/2026` with the day or the month first, and written-out dates
  like `Jan 5, 2026`.
- **Amounts**: `-12.00`, `$1,234.56` or `1.234,56`, and `(12.00)` or `12.00-` for money going
  out. With separate inflow and outflow columns, FlyBudget works out the sign.
- **Separators**: columns separated by commas or by semicolons.
- **Excel files**: `.xlsx` and the older `.xls`, read on your device like a CSV file. When a
  workbook has several sheets, FlyBudget opens the first one that holds transactions, and you can
  pick another under **Sheet**. Password-protected workbooks can't be read.
- **Headers** in English or Spanish (`Fecha`, `Concepto`, `Débito`, `Crédito`, `Importe`).
- **Statements with details on top**: when the bank writes the account holder, number or period
  above the column headers, FlyBudget starts at the headers and leaves those lines out.
- **Accents**: older bank files (not UTF-8) are read too, so `Número` and `Débito` show properly.
- Files up to 100,000 rows. Rows with an amount of zero are left out.

## Dates and decimals

Banks don't agree on how to write a date or an amount, so the import has two choices:

- **Dates**: **Day first** (`31/12/2026`) or **Month first** (`12/31/2026`). `05/01/2026` is
  5 January under the first and 1 May under the second.
- **Decimals**: **Comma** (`1.234,56`) or **Point** (`1,234.56`).

FlyBudget guesses both from the file: a date with a day above 12 shows the order, and an amount
with cents shows the decimal mark. When nothing in the file tells (every day is 12 or lower, no
amount has cents), it picks month first and point, so check the choices the first time you import
from a bank. Once an import succeeds, FlyBudget remembers the choices for that account on this
device and uses them for its next file, along with how you mapped the columns (for files with the
same headers).

In an Excel file, amounts stored as numbers are read exactly whatever you choose; the choices
apply to dates and amounts the file stores as text.

A row whose date or amount can't be read under your choices is never imported as a wrong day or
a wrong amount. It's listed in the dialog with what couldn't be read, and left out. If many rows
are listed, one of the two choices is probably wrong: change it, and the file is read again
straight away, on the preview too.

## Credit cards

**Purchases written as positive numbers.** Many card statements write a purchase as `1.234,56`
and a payment or refund as `-1.234,56`, the opposite of what an account needs. Tick **Purchases
are positive in this file** under the columns: every sign is turned around, so purchases are
money going out and payments and refunds are money coming in. For a credit card account FlyBudget
ticks it for you when most of the file's amounts are positive. Check that the first row reads the
way you expect.

**Pesos and dollars in one file.** A card that works in both currencies is two accounts in
FlyBudget, one in pesos and one in dollars, and its statement lists both. FlyBudget reads the two
ways banks write that:

- a column for each currency (`Pesos` and `Dólares`, or `Importe $` and `Importe U$S`), mapped as
  **Amount in pesos** and **Amount in dollars**;
- one amount column and a column that names each row's currency (`Moneda`: `Pesos` or `Dólares`),
  mapped as **Amount** and **Currency**.

Open either of the card's two accounts and import the file there. The rows in that account's
currency go into it. For the others, the dialog shows **Rows in dollars go to** (or **Rows in
pesos go to**) with the accounts of that currency:

- When the account is in an Account Group that has exactly one other account of the same type in
  the other currency, that account is already chosen.
- Otherwise it starts on **Not imported**, and you choose. FlyBudget never guesses between two
  accounts.

The preview shows which account each row goes to, and the amount in that account's currency.
Nothing is converted: a dollar purchase is saved as dollars. FlyBudget remembers the account you
chose for the next file. Importing the same file again from the other account is safe: every row
is found as a duplicate.
